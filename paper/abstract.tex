Exact verifiers identify invalid model outputs, but richer feedback need not improve performance beyond simply revising a previous candidate. We study this distinction using directed-path construction and Boolean-program synthesis with complete, executable checks. A frozen protocol evaluates three local model configurations on 48 development tasks and 256 deduplicated test tasks across six difficulty strata. Development selects the strongest configuration separately for independent sampling, validity-only refinement, and exact-feedback refinement. All matched strategies receive three calls with identical completion-token caps; mixed-model controls use the same budgets. Exact-feedback refinement solves 117/256 tasks, compared with 115/256 for validity-only refinement and 78/256 for independent sampling. Mixed-model refinement solves 106/256. Neither conservative primary interval establishes the prespecified five-percentage-point advantage. A serving abort in a secondary mixed-model trajectory independently activates the protocol's global exclusion rule; one separately amended exact-payload retry also fails. Exact classical algorithms solve all 256 tasks. The audited results therefore support a diagnostic comparison rather than a superiority claim. They show why repeated revision, informative verification, model diversity, resource accounting, and operational failures must be separated when evaluating model collaboration.
